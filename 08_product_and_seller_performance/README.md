## 1. Top Seller Performance (Revenue Leaders)
A small group of sellers drives significant revenue, but their strategies differ:
* Some sellers generate revenue through high volume (e.g., 1700+ orders with lower AOV)
* Others rely on high-value orders (AOV > 500 with fewer transactions)

**Insight:**
Top sellers are not uniform—there are two clear business models: Volume-driven sellers (scale-focused) & Value-driven sellers (premium-focused). This indicates a diverse marketplace structure, rather than a single dominant selling pattern.


## 2. Category–Seller Concentration
* **Health & Beauty** leads with **~₹1.23M** revenue across 479 sellers (~₹2.5K per seller), indicating a highly competitive category.
* **Watches** generates **~₹1.16M** revenue with only 95 sellers (~₹12.3K per seller), showing strong concentration.
* **PCs** is extremely concentrated: **~₹218K** revenue with just 9 sellers **(~₹24K per seller)**
* **Office Furniture** (~₹268K, 30 sellers, ~₹8.9K per seller) also reflects high per-seller dominance.

**Insight:**
Mass categories (e.g., Health & Beauty) → many sellers, distributed revenue. Niche/high-value categories (e.g., PCs, Watches) → fewer sellers capturing a large share. This indicates uneven competitive intensity across categories.


## 3. Seller–Category Dependency
Several niche categories are heavily dominated by a single seller:
* cds music dvds → 100% control
* La Cuisine → 94%
* PCs → ~75%

Mid-tier categories show moderate dominance:
* Office Furniture → 64%
* Home Appliances → 44%

Large categories like Health & Beauty (~6%) and Sports/Leisure (~5.5%) are highly distributed with no single seller dominance.

**Insight:**
There is a clear split between:
* Highly concentrated niche categories (single-seller dependency)
* Mature competitive categories (distributed seller base)

This indicates varying levels of market maturity and competitive intensity across categories.
